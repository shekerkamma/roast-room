# Principles: Justin Welsh

Built from the extract notes for S001 to S083 (his weekly essays, January 2022 to October 2026). 28 decision rules, most-used first, weighted toward solo business and content-led growth. "Sources" is the number of distinct sources that state or apply the rule (ids listed). Confidence: high = 3 or more sources, medium = 2, low = 1.

1. **Choose the life over the bigger business; decide what enough is before scale decides for you.** "Scale is the default. Enough is a decision." (S041) "Will this business decision make my life better? Or just make my business bigger?" (S052) Sources: 7 (S007, S011, S029, S041, S045, S047, S052). Confidence: high. Changed over time: he says he would have jumped at a partner deal "three years ago" and now passes (S052, 2024-12-28); by 2026 he accepts falling revenue for enough (S029, 2026-04-11). The newer view governs.

2. **If it makes the business harder to run, it does not ship.** Every custom request, hire or partner adds work someone must remember and manage. "It couldn’t make the business harder to run." (S004) "I didn’t want to manage any employees." (S007) Sources: 6 (S004, S007, S041, S048, S052, S075). Confidence: high.

3. **Start with the simplest version and add complexity only after it fails.** "But making a change after a product or service isn't working is very different from adding complexity to it before anyone's even asked." (S004) Sources: 6 (S004, S022, S040, S053, S055, S070). Confidence: high.

4. **Build attention before you build revenue.** "Become a master at getting attention and build an audience before you worry about making any money." (S047) "Most people try selling courses way too early, instead of focusing on building their audience first." (S079) Sources: 5 (S017, S047, S059, S061, S079). Confidence: high.

5. **Let chosen numbers decide, not fast opinions.** "I don't let the fast comments dictate what I do next. I let the numbers do that." (S020) "Somebody can tell me my website is ugly, but ugly (or pretty) isn't on the list of things I’m measuring." (S020) Sources: 5 (S020, S058, S064, S071, S074). Confidence: high.

6. **Past basic competence, visibility beats more skill.** "The market doesn't reward the most talented people. It rewards talented people who are also visible." (S017) "You can’t afford to be a bystander if you hope to make a name for yourself." (S059) Sources: 4 (S017, S054, S059, S061). Confidence: high. Scope: he limits this to getting started (S017).

7. **Ship the scrappy version; perfect costs growth.** "today’s scrappy version beats the polished version you're waiting for." (S040) "Because perfect isn't just the enemy of good. It's the enemy of growth." (S050) Sources: 4 (S040, S050, S054, S070). Confidence: high.

8. **Ask for the sale, often, and make buying easy.** "One of the key mistakes I see solopreneurs making is their hesitancy to ask for the business." (S083) "Make it easy for people to buy from you." (S070) Sources: 4 (S013, S070, S074, S083). Confidence: high.

9. **Raise prices on proven value until your voice cracks.** "Then package your offer at a price that makes your voice crack a little." (S046) "Once you’ve accumulated several testimonials, raise your rates, and turn those early customers into case studies." (S075) Sources: 4 (S006, S013, S046, S075). Confidence: high. Applies most clearly to scarce one-to-one time.

10. **Profitability over size; do not raise money.** "Does this make the business more profitable or just bigger?" (S047) "The moment you raise capital, you get a boss." (S047) Sources: 4 (S007, S011, S041, S047). Confidence: high.

11. **Automate operations, never the creative, roadmap or pricing.** "But don't automate your creative, your product roadmap, or your pricing." (S047) Sources: 4 (S016, S047, S048, S078). Confidence: high.

12. **Keep fixed costs near zero and stay off paid ads until $1 returns $2.** "Stay away from ads and expensive marketing campaigns." (S068) "This means having healthy margins, keeping fixed costs low, and having enough runway to get through slow periods." (S047) "I’ve never paid for impressions, engagement, or subscribers." (S061) Sources: 4 (S047, S061, S068, S070). Confidence: high.

13. **Talk to customers instead of investors; learn the objections from them.** "Instead of pitching investors, get really good at talking to customers." (S047) "Remember to talk to prospects to find out why they haven’t moved forward with other solutions in the past." (S057) Sources: 4 (S047, S057, S063, S070). Confidence: high.

14. **Consistency and systems beat occasional brilliance.** "Because boring, executed systematically, becomes extraordinary." (S051) "A good system can save you." (S066) Sources: 4 (S051, S065, S066, S068). Confidence: high.

15. **Follow curiosity; when obligation replaces it, the work shows it.** "My own curiosity has continued to be the signal." (S042) "What saved me was letting go of the stuff that made strategic sense on paper, and writing what I really cared about instead." (S035) Sources: 4 (S001, S016, S035, S042). Confidence: high. Changed over time: early essays (2022 to 2023) are tactical LinkedIn playbooks; from 2025 he writes what he cares about and says the detour earns less (S035, 2026-02-28).

16. **For products sold to a broad audience, a low entry price builds trust and word of mouth.** "The future is $50, not $1,500." (S082) "Lower prices beat higher ones because more people have access. And they tell more people." (S053) Sources: 3 (S053, S075, S082). Confidence: high. Tension with rule 9 over time: low price stated in 2022 (S082) and 2024 (S053), while his 2024 flagship averaged $566 a sale (S058) and his 2025 essay praises a 7x consulting price (S046). Inferred from S046, S053, S058, S082: cheap entry products, premium scarce time, and he never calls it a reversal.

17. **Eliminate before you simplify, automate or delegate; say no.** "eliminate these meetings and learn to say no moving forward." (S078) "For the other 80% of tasks, you can eliminate, simplify, automate, or delegate" (S069) Sources: 3 (S069, S073, S078). Confidence: high.

18. **Content exists to drive revenue, not applause.** "Obvious Content Tells. Non-obvious Content Sells." (S077) "My goal is never to go viral." (S080) Sources: 3 (S055, S077, S080). Confidence: high.

19. **Own the audience: move followers to email.** "email marketing still greatly outperforms social media content when it comes to converting readers to customers." (S056) "Make sure a large portion of your social content has links to newsletter issues." (S062) Sources: 3 (S056, S062, S073). Confidence: high.

20. **Scrutinize what a good-looking offer asks you to give up.** "But any good-looking opportunity that asks for something valuable of yours in return deserves a lot of scrutiny." (S008) Loneliness is not a reason to give away equity (S033). Sources: 3 (S008, S033, S041). Confidence: high.

21. **Test small on the side, then commit with conviction; do not wait for fear to pass.** "Then, once you've got the aim down, you fire with conviction." (S027) "Nobody I know has ever done a hard thing after the fear completely subsided." (S027) Sources: 3 (S001, S006, S027). Confidence: high.

22. **Deferring is a decision; make it on purpose and early.** "Schedule it or admit you're not doing it." (S044) "People deserve bad news while it can still help them." (S010) Sources: 3 (S010, S036, S044). Confidence: high.

23. **Methods rarely transfer; trust your own judgment over a successful person's playbook.** "But methods don't always transfer. Principles usually do." (S053) "Anything important that’s worth understanding is worth the full version." (S018) Sources: 3 (S018, S024, S053). Confidence: high.

24. **Do not rebuild corporate life inside your own business.** "If you're going to walk away from corporate life, don't recreate it in your own business." (S045) Sources: 3 (S007, S045, S073). Confidence: high.

25. **Give a test enough time before you judge it.** "don’t jump to conclusions based on a single metric or a small amount of time." (S064) His 7x price had no takers for three months, then four clients by month six (S046); his launch lull looked like failure and was normal (S043). Sources: 3 (S043, S046, S064). Confidence: high.

26. **Speak to where each customer actually is.** "Your audience isn't one person with one problem." (S043) "A good product deserves an equally thoughtful launch to engage the right customers and prepare them to buy." (S058) Sources: 2 (S043, S058). Confidence: medium.

27. **Optimize only what carries the revenue; leave the rest at good enough.** "You've gotta leave something on the table." (S050) Sources: 2 (S050, S069). Confidence: medium. Changed over time: he pushed A/B tests and page rewrites in 2024 (S057, S058) and in 2025 wrote "Hell, I've preached most of this stuff myself." (S050). The newer view governs.

28. **Build the schedule and environment, not willpower.** "In the long run, your environment will almost always win against your willpower." (S049) "The freedom is the freedom to choose your constraints. Not the freedom from constraints." (S025) Sources: 2 (S025, S049). Confidence: medium.
