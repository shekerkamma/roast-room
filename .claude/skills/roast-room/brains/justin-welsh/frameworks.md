# Frameworks: Justin Welsh

Built from the extract notes for S001 to S083 (his weekly essays, January 2022 to October 2026). 25 frameworks, roughly most-used first, weighted toward solo business and content-led growth. "Sources" counts distinct sources that use the idea (ids listed). Names are his where he named them; otherwise a short descriptive name marked *(unnamed)*. The How-to-score lines are scoring guidance for the council built from his stated thresholds, not quotes.

---

## 1. Life-first business: written non-negotiables, anti-goals, and enough *(unnamed as a whole; "anti-goals" and "enough" are his words)*
- **What it is:** Decide in writing how you want the business to work, and what enough money is, before opportunities arrive. Then test every opportunity against that list, not against revenue.
- **Parts:** Write specific rules (his 2020 list: no employees to manage, no more than 30 hours a week, most Fridays off, no more than two meetings a day, no meeting over 45 minutes, no work on family evenings or weekends) (S007). Run anti-goals: comb the calendar for what drains you, write "I won't" statements, then the how (S073). Define hours first and fit operations inside them; set an enough number and optimize to sustain it, not double it (S052). Ask whether a choice makes life better or only the business bigger.
- **When to use:** Any growth offer: hiring, ads, a team, a community, events, partnerships, a bigger launch.
- **How to score:** A move that breaks a written rule fails even if it doubles revenue. A move that adds revenue inside the rules passes. A founder with no written rules or enough number scores low on readiness to decide at all.
- **Quote:** "Will this business decision make my life better? Or just make my business bigger?" (S052)
- **Changed over time:** In 2022 and 2023 he wrote about growth phases, paid ads and a future project manager (S068, 2023-06-17). By late 2024 he says he would have taken a partner deal three years earlier and now passes (S052, 2024-12-28). In 2026 he says revenue fell after he stopped promoting most of what built the business, and he accepts it: "For the first time in a long time, I’m not focused on maximizing revenue. I'm focused on enough." (S029, 2026-04-11). The newer view is enough first.
- **Sources:** 8 (S007, S011, S029, S041, S045, S047, S052, S073)


## 2. Swing the axe: exposure before polish, attention before revenue *(unnamed; image from S017)*
- **What it is:** Past basic competence, the bottleneck for a new solo business is that nobody knows you exist. Put yourself in front of many relevant people daily instead of improving the craft or the product.
- **Parts:** Post every day on one platform (LinkedIn first for him); do podcasts, DMs, cold emails; promote your thinking, not the product; build an audience before you sell; start small and do it scared.
- **When to use:** A founder with expertise and no pipeline; a founder about to build a course, product or community for an audience that does not exist yet.
- **How to score:** High if the founder already ships content daily to a defined audience. Low if the plan is to perfect the offer first, or to sell a course before any audience. His starting challenge is 30 days of daily self-promotion (S059).
- **Quote:** "you don't get a one-in-a-thousand break without taking a thousand swings first." (S017)
- **Limits:** He scopes it to the start: "I just don't think that's true when you're getting started as an entrepreneur." (S017). In the AI era he also argues the opposite tactic for job seekers, one high-effort attempt aimed at one person, because volume became free (S014, 2026-07-25). Inferred from S014, S017: volume wins for audience building, aim wins where AI makes volume worthless.
- **Sources:** 7 (S014, S017, S047, S054, S059, S061, S079)


## 3. Try the easy version first *(his phrase in S004; "ugliest possible version" in S040)*
- **What it is:** Launch the simplest version that could work and add complexity only after it fails, never before anyone asked.
- **Parts:** Close the tabs of conflicting advice; sketch your own simple version; set the terms yourself (one page with price, placement and rules; a buy button; an automated email); refuse custom requests that someone must remember and manage; change it only when results say so.
- **When to use:** Designing an offer, a sponsorship or service process, a funnel, a course structure, or learning a new tool.
- **How to score:** Pass if it can run with no new staff and no meetings. Fail if the first version already has tiers, custom deals, or tooling nobody has asked for. His first product: about 90 hours, webcam and self-made slides, $50, one LinkedIn post (S040).
- **Quote:** "Instead, find out whether the simplest version works first." (S004)
- **Sources:** 6 (S004, S022, S040, S053, S055, S070)


## 4. Pivot tests: half the money, curiosity as signal, the thing you'd do anyway *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** A set of questions for when a working business has stopped being enjoyable or interesting.
- **Parts:** Would you take the same money from this business, or half from something you are excited about (S001)? Is curiosity rising, or has obligation replaced it (S042)? Is this the thing you would do anyway if nobody paid you (S016)? Why do you actually want the old thing back: identity, money, safety, loneliness (S033)? Move gradually: charge more, take fewer clients, test on the side.
- **When to use:** A founder bored by a profitable line, or tempted to return to a past role or offer.
- **How to score:** Guidance: if the honest answer is half the money for the exciting thing and there is a financial floor, favor a gradual move; if there are dependants and no floor, staying can be rational (S001).
- **Quote:** "Would you rather make the same money you make today running the business you have now, or half of what you make building something you're genuinely excited about?" (S001)
- **Sources:** 6 (S001, S009, S016, S033, S035, S042)


## 5. Content reuse system: templates, the 730-day library, remixing winners *(his names: "730-day content library"; "Level II power-up")*
- **What it is:** Treat content as a system, not inspiration. Grade what you publish, turn winners into templates, and recycle them across time and platforms.
- **Parts:** Brain-dump sub-topics, list 5 to 7 content styles, build a sub-topic by style matrix; log every post with a 1 to 10 grade; recycle winners every 6 months; study your top 20 posts and rewrite each many ways across formats; template every winning structure.
- **When to use:** A founder whose content plan depends on daily inspiration, or who throws posts away after one use.
- **How to score:** High if there is a log with grades and a reuse schedule; low if each post starts from a blank page.
- **Quote:** "For every post that I create that performs well, I analyze the post, turn it into a template, and reuse it multiple times." (S080)
- **Sources:** 5 (S051, S061, S065, S066, S080)


## 6. Eliminate, Simplify, Automate, Delegate
- **What it is:** A weekly pass over the calendar, in a fixed order, so a solo operator keeps only the work that moves the goal.
- **Parts:** Eliminate (does this move me closer to my most important goals?); Simplify (smaller scope, minimum version; his content system went from 12 to 15 hours a week to about 7); Automate (onboarding with Kajabi, Airtable, Zapier); Delegate (a virtual assistant with process docs). Order matters. Automate operations but never your creative, roadmap or pricing (S047).
- **When to use:** Founder overload, support load outrunning creation, or a plan to hire to fix a messy business.
- **How to score:** A plan that hires before eliminating and simplifying scores low. A plan that automates pricing or creative decisions scores low.
- **Quote:** "Does this move me closer to my most important goals?" (S078)
- **Sources:** 5 (S016, S047, S048, S069, S078)


## 7. Content-to-cash funnel *(his names: "basic marketing funnel", "engagement loop", "stages of customer awareness")*
- **What it is:** Content builds attention, email builds trust, a page makes the sale. Each stage has a job and you do not sell at the top.
- **Parts:** Awareness (help, do not sell); Interest (a lead magnet that solves a small problem that unlocks a bigger one); Consideration (a nurture sequence: story, case studies, answers); Intent (the offer, with a limited-time price or seats); Evaluation (FAQ, comparisons, guarantee) (S055). Route social posts to newsletter issues and back (engagement loop, S062). Content mix by awareness: about 50% to the problem-unaware, about 40% agitating the problem, long form for the solution, a no-brainer offer for the most aware (S067).
- **When to use:** A founder with audience traction and weak sales, or followers but no email list.
- **How to score:** Low if there is no email capture; low if the funnel sells the moment it gets the address or goes silent after. High if each stage exists, even in simple form.
- **Quote:** "Because a simple funnel is better than no funnel at all." (S055)
- **Sources:** 5 (S055, S056, S062, S067, S075)


## 8. Two-speed feedback: numbers over opinions *(his name: "the two-speed problem")*
- **What it is:** Opinions arrive fast and loud; results arrive slow and reliable. Decide by results you chose in advance.
- **Parts:** Pick one or two key results; write hypotheses and rank them by expected impact; ship one; watch the numbers; keep it if significantly better, undo and review if worse. Ask whether criticism is aligned with where you are going, not whether it is right. Use a baseline (his $0.00112 revenue per impression in S074) and controlled tests (8.69% versus 12.01% purchase rate in S058).
- **When to use:** A redesign, a launch, an algorithm change, or a wave of public criticism.
- **How to score:** Low if the founder is changing course because of comments or a single bad week; high if there is a stated metric and a test window.
- **Quote:** "I don't let the fast comments dictate what I do next. I let the numbers do that." (S020)
- **Sources:** 5 (S020, S058, S064, S071, S074)


## 9. What if it works? *(his question in S007)*
- **What it is:** After running the numbers and imagining failure, imagine full success and ask whether you want to run that business.
- **Parts:** Picture the team, office, meetings and stress the win creates; list what you get (easy to measure) and what you give up (hard to measure) (S008); ask whether stripping it back to you and one other person would be better and more enjoyable (S029).
- **When to use:** Partnerships, co-founder offers, equity deals, hiring a team, adding ads or events.
- **How to score:** Fail if success produces a business the founder's own rules reject, whatever the money. He backed out of a SaaS co-founder plan after picturing it (S041).
- **Quote:** "An opportunity can work exactly as promised and give you a business you don’t want to run." (S007)
- **Sources:** 4 (S007, S008, S029, S041)


## 10. Trust tripwire and value ladder (low price first)
- **What it is:** Sell a cheap, short, high-value product first to build trust and volume, then offer the higher-priced service or course to buyers.
- **Parts:** Price the tripwire under $100; keep a course 45 to 60 minutes; one specific outcome; value over production; turn buyers into affiliates (S082). Value ladder: a low-cost course first, the done-for-you service positioned at the middle and end of the course (S075).
- **When to use:** A creator with a modest audience choosing between a high-ticket course and a cheap one.
- **How to score:** Guidance from his numbers: without social capital, a high-ticket first product scores low. His ladder example: 3 courses a day at $50 plus 10% taking a $450 service gives about $101,400 a year (S075).
- **Quote:** "The future is $50, not $1,500." (S082)
- **Changed over time:** Low price was his public stance in 2022 (S082, 2022-02-12) and again in 2024 (S053, 2024-11-23). His later products went up: the Creator MBA sold at a $566 average transaction (S058, 2024-06-01), and in 2025 he wrote about charging $14,400 for eight hours of consulting (S046, 2025-07-26; the story is from his consulting years). He never states a reversal. Inferred from S046, S053, S058, S082: low price for products to a broad audience, high price for scarce one-to-one time, and higher course prices once he had the audience.
- **Sources:** 4 (S053, S072, S075, S082)


## 11. FITI method (Feedback, Iteration, Testimonials, Increase)
- **What it is:** How he justified a 7x price rise on consulting.
- **Parts:** Email every past-year client three questions (where did I create the most value, what do you want more of, what felt like a waste); cut what clients never mention and you dislike; collect testimonials with hard numbers; package one focused offer at the new price. Raise rates once testimonials exist (S075); let higher rates cut hours rather than refill them (S006).
- **When to use:** A service founder who is busy, undercharging, and hiding behind hourly billing.
- **How to score:** High if there is proof with metrics and one focused offer; expect a slow start (his first three months at the new price had no new client, S046).
- **Quote:** "Then package your offer at a price that makes your voice crack a little." (S046)
- **Sources:** 4 (S006, S013, S046, S075)


## 12. Copy formulas: PASTOR, 4P, the viral opener, the thread system
- **What it is:** Reusable structures for promotional and audience content.
- **Parts:** PASTOR (Problem, Amplify, Story, Transformation, Offer, Response; Ray Edwards's method, S060). 4P (Promise, Picture, Proof, Push, S072). Viral opener: a relatable enemy, the hero, gasoline plus a teaser, inside the first 210 characters (S080). Threads: write the body, then the call to action, then the hook last (S081).
- **When to use:** Founders who write content that gets likes but no signups or sales.
- **How to score:** Low if a post has no call to action or a hook written first; high if each piece ends with a clear next step.
- **Quote:** "The first line is more important than 95% of the rest of the post." (S080)
- **Sources:** 4 (S060, S072, S080, S081)


## 13. Starter models for a solo side business
- **What it is:** His recommended first businesses and the order to try them.
- **Parts:** Service business first (easiest start; 3x to 5x your day-job hourly rate; hard to scale without a team), then a digital course (needs traction first), paid community (most powerful, hardest), subscription email (scales, churns if you miss) (S079). Anthony Natoli's nine steps and the 30-30-30 method: 30 minutes a week talking to prospects, 30 a day solving their problems, 30 a day creating content about them (S070). His tweet path: take what you do at work, a simple landing page, a book-a-call button, 90 days of content (S063).
- **When to use:** Someone still employed choosing what to sell first.
- **How to score:** High if the first offer uses a skill the person knows cold and needs no employees; low if the first product is a community or a big course with no audience.
- **Quote:** "Most people try selling courses way too early, instead of focusing on building their audience first." (S079)
- **Sources:** 4 (S063, S070, S075, S079)


## 14. Anti-VC playbook and the profit test
- **What it is:** Nine rules to stay solo, profitable and in control.
- **Parts:** Start with lifestyle in mind; build attention before revenue; profitability over growth; automate operations, not strategy; several revenue streams; master customer development; keep optionality (no long contracts you cannot exit); plan for plateaus; network with solopreneurs, not startup founders. Every feature, hire and decision passes one question.
- **When to use:** A founder weighing funding, a co-founder, a team, or growth for its own sake.
- **How to score:** Unit economics must work at 10 customers and at 10,000 (S047). A choice that only makes the business bigger fails.
- **Quote:** "Does this make the business more profitable or just bigger?" (S047)
- **Limits:** He is fair to the other path: "Some businesses and founders want to raise a bunch of capital. There's nothing wrong with that." (S047)
- **Sources:** 3 (S011, S041, S047)


## 15. Fire bullets, then cannonballs *(Jim Collins's idea, which he adopts)*
- **What it is:** Test small and on the side until you have evidence, then make the big move with conviction.
- **Parts:** Run the new thing alongside the old; read the evidence; commit fully once aimed. His own move: he shut down two courses making 75% of revenue only after a year of pivoting (S027). Free time from higher rates became his first course (S006).
- **When to use:** Quitting a job, killing a main product line, or moving into a new market.
- **How to score:** High if there is side evidence before the leap; low for a blind leap or for waiting until fear is gone.
- **Quote:** "Then, once you've got the aim down, you fire with conviction." (S027)
- **Sources:** 3 (S001, S006, S027)


## 16. Ask for the business *(his name: the ask day is unnamed; "ask" is his word)*
- **What it is:** Selling is a regular part of content, not an afterthought, and buyers need repeated reminders.
- **Parts:** Spend 10% to 20% of content time on offers; make buying easy and put the link everywhere (S070); ask high and specific (S013).
- **When to use:** A founder with audience and a product who rarely pitches or calls it passive income.
- **How to score:** His numbers: ask days $10K to $20K against about $2,500 on passive days, a 5x to 10x difference (S083).
- **Quote:** "One of the key mistakes I see solopreneurs making is their hesitancy to ask for the business." (S083)
- **Sources:** 3 (S013, S070, S083)


## 17. Segmented launch *(his persona name: "The Struggling Side Hustler")*
- **What it is:** Do not write one launch message for the whole list. Ask subscribers where they are and personalize a small part of every email.
- **Parts:** Interest list page; a survey on the thank-you page (business stage, income source, platform, challenge); weekly emails while building; a baseline launch sequence; extra emails per segment; 5% to 10% of each email swapped by segment; a personalized sales-page header.
- **When to use:** A launch to a list with mixed stages, or a higher-priced product.
- **How to score:** His results: a 38% lift worth about $650,000 on a $1.6M launch (S043, S058). Low if the founder plans to wing the launch; his winged 2022 launch did about $55K against a $200K to $250K expectation (S043).
- **Quote:** "I just stopped trying to talk to everyone the same way." (S043)
- **Sources:** 2 (S043, S058)


## 18. Cost of optimization: the 80/20 decision and the impact-to-time ratio
- **What it is:** Before spending time improving something, compare the real revenue impact to the real cost, including opportunity cost and time away from family.
- **Parts:** Estimate impact from actual revenue; estimate cost; act only if impact dwarfs cost (S050). Task audit: score each task 1 to 10 for impact, divide by weekly hours, keep the top 20%, eliminate, simplify, automate or delegate the rest; repeat twice a year (S069).
- **When to use:** A founder polishing a sales page, A/B testing small funnels, or drowning in tasks.
- **How to score:** His worked cases: 20 hours for $150 a month is not worth it; 20 hours for $1,200 a month is a careful maybe; 20 hours for $4,000 a month is worth it (S050).
- **Quote:** "Now ask yourself: Does the impact absolutely dwarf the cost?" (S050)
- **Changed over time:** In 2024 he pushed controlled tests and page rewrites (S057, S058). In 2025 he says: "Hell, I've preached most of this stuff myself." (S050, 2025-03-08). The newer view is to optimize only the core product, the main page and onboarding.
- **Sources:** 2 (S050, S069)


## 19. Floor before ceiling
- **What it is:** Use money to build a floor (what holds when something breaks) before a visible ceiling (cars, house, lifestyle). The floor buys optionality.
- **Parts:** Keep lifestyle below what you can afford; plan for taxes; keep fixed costs low and runway for slow periods (S047).
- **When to use:** Spending decisions after a good year; deciding whether you can afford to walk away from a client or line.
- **How to score:** High if a bad quarter would not force a bad decision; low if fixed costs rise with every revenue gain.
- **Quote:** "That’s what building a floor actually buys you. Optionality." (S019)
- **Sources:** 2 (S019, S047)


## 20. The 5 phases of solopreneurship
- **What it is:** A stage map for a solo business, each phase a year or two.
- **Parts:** Stay Alive (low expenses, free social, no ads); Build Systems (document every repeated task); Growth Focused (add platforms, newsletter, partnerships; he spent 2.5 years on LinkedIn only); Invest in Defense (brand, SEO, automation as copycats arrive); Automate the Business (paid ads when $1 produces $2, a part-time project manager).
- **When to use:** Placing a founder's question in time, especially ads, tooling and hiring.
- **How to score:** Ads before the business can turn $1 into $2 score low; expensive software and branding in Phase 1 score low.
- **Quote:** "Ultimately, you want to get to a place where you’re spending $1 to generate $2." (S068)
- **Limits:** "I haven’t reached this phase yet, so I can’t speak on it as intelligently as the first four phases." (S068). By 2026 he still runs with no employees (S041), so the Phase 5 hire never happened.
- **Sources:** 1 (S068)


## 21. Creator versus operator
- **What it is:** Creators live for ideas and audience; operators live for systems and metrics. A solo business needs both.
- **Parts:** Be honest about which you are; spend about a day a week on the other side; design around your strengths with one of four moves: partner, hire, automate, or stay intentionally small. The game changes around $250K when support and fires outrun creation.
- **When to use:** A creator business that is growing and getting messier, or an operator with no pipeline.
- **How to score:** His thresholds: pure creators end at a $350K mess, pure operators at a $50K ceiling. More creation without better operations scores low.
- **Quote:** "Know what you're good at, and design your business to work with you, not against you." (S048)
- **Sources:** 1 (S048)


## 22. The 5-4-3-2-1 method (landing page)
- **What it is:** A sales page checklist.
- **Parts:** 5 benefits; 4 objections answered (learned by talking to prospects); 3 testimonials covering different objections; 2 pricing options for anchoring; 1 bonus stacked on the offer. Optionally test it in reverse order.
- **When to use:** A page with traffic and a good product but weak conversion.
- **How to score:** A client saw a 27% lift in on-page conversion. A page with one yes/no price and no objections handled scores low.
- **Quote:** "A landing page has one job: To convert interest into sales." (S057)
- **Sources:** 1 (S057)


## 23. The 5 levels of pain, priority, and timing (discovery call)
- **What it is:** A discovery-call structure that builds a business case instead of pitching.
- **Parts:** Surface pain; historical pain (what was tried); financial pain (put dollars on it); priority (is it number one); timing (ASAP, a date, or vague). Then a written case: what you heard, how you fix it, price, timeline.
- **When to use:** Selling a service or high-ticket offer one to one.
- **How to score:** Low if the founder pitches features on the first call; high if price is shown against the dollar loss (his example: a $10K fix against a $36K yearly loss).
- **Quote:** "If you jump on a Zoom and just start pitching, you’re almost never going to close the deal." (S063)
- **Sources:** 1 (S063)


## 24. Simplify the purchase at peak engagement *(unnamed; from the Blake Burge case)*
- **What it is:** Sell at the moment of highest interest instead of routing people through free downloads and long nurture.
- **Parts:** Look for friction, leakage and complication in the sales path; charge for the thing rather than giving it away; offer it at the end of the content; upsell by email; track revenue per impression as a baseline.
- **When to use:** Lower-ticket digital products with long free-to-paid paths.
- **How to score:** His case: $29.40 per thread became $2,243, 76x the average. He limits it to lower-ticket items.
- **Quote:** "Free products are easy to ignore because there is no skin in the game." (S074)
- **Sources:** 1 (S074)


## 25. The 5-Day Decision Rule
- **What it is:** For anything deferred three months or more, decide within five days: schedule it like an urgent client meeting, or say out loud you will not do it and delegate or drop it.
- **Parts:** List what you keep saying you will do soon; ask what admitting you will never do it says about you; within five days schedule it or drop it. He applies it to pricing changes, hard conversations and hires.
- **When to use:** A founder who keeps postponing a price change, a hire, or a hard talk.
- **How to score:** His example cost: a consultant delaying value-based pricing loses about $5,000 a month (S044).
- **Quote:** "Schedule it or admit you're not doing it." (S044)
- **Sources:** 1 (S044)
