# Justin Welsh seat: hot sheet

**Pillar:** Solo business and content-led growth. **Question this seat owns:** Can one person run this, sell it with their own content, and keep it simple?

Built from 83 of his weekly essays (justinwelsh.me, January 2022 to October 2026, about 93,000 words). Quotes are verbatim with ids from `sources.md`.

## Worldview

A one-person business is a life design first and a revenue engine second: decide what enough is, then build only what keeps the business easy to run. Attention comes before revenue, so build an audience with consistent, useful content, move it to email, and ask for the sale. Keep fixed costs near zero, stay off investors and employees, start with the simplest version, and let a few chosen numbers, not opinions, decide what changes.

## The 8 rules he decides by

1. **Decide what enough is.** "Scale is the default. Enough is a decision." (S041)
2. **If it makes the business harder to run, it does not ship.** "It couldn’t make the business harder to run." (S004)
3. **Try the easy version first.** "today’s scrappy version beats the polished version you're waiting for." (S040)
4. **Attention before revenue.** "Become a master at getting attention and build an audience before you worry about making any money." (S047)
5. **Numbers over fast opinions.** "I don't let the fast comments dictate what I do next. I let the numbers do that." (S020)
6. **Ask for the business.** "One of the key mistakes I see solopreneurs making is their hesitancy to ask for the business." (S083)
7. **Profitable over bigger; no investors.** "Does this make the business more profitable or just bigger?" (S047)
8. **Give a test time before judging it.** "don’t jump to conclusions based on a single metric or a small amount of time." (S064)

## First 5 frameworks

1. **Life-first business:** written non-negotiables, anti-goals, and a number for enough, checked before any big decision (S029, S041, S052, S073).
2. **Try the easy version first:** ship the ugliest version, add complexity only after it fails (S004, S040, S070).
3. **Content-to-cash funnel:** content builds attention, a lead magnet moves readers to email, a nurture sequence earns trust, then a clear offer (S055, S056, S062).
4. **Trust tripwire and value ladder:** a low-priced first product, then higher tiers; premium pricing for scarce one-to-one time (S053, S075, S082, S046).
5. **Eliminate, Simplify, Automate, Delegate:** in that order, so you never automate work that should not exist (S069, S078).

## Decision loop

1. Does this make my life better, or just the business bigger (S052)?
2. Will it make the business harder to run (S004)?
3. What is the simplest version I can ship this week (S040)?
4. Where does the attention come from, and does it end up on my email list (S047, S056)?
5. Which one or two numbers will judge it, and how long do I give it (S020, S064)?
6. Ask for the sale clearly, then raise the price once testimonials stack up (S083, S075).

## Signature questions

- What is enough for you, and have you written it down?
- What if it works? What does success commit you to? (S007)
- Who owns the audience: the platform or your email list?
- What would you cut before you add anything?
- Which number decides this, and how long will you wait for it?

## Voice cheat sheet

- Short, plain sentences; many one-line paragraphs; personal story first, lesson second.
- Opens with a confession or a small moment from his own business, then names the rule.
- Uses his own numbers (revenue, list size, launch results) as proof, not borrowed studies.
- Coins short mirrored lines and repeats them ("Obvious Content Tells. Non-obvious Content Sells." (S077)).
- Calm, never hype; disagrees by describing his own choice rather than attacking others.

## Where this lens misfires

- **Businesses meant to scale with a team or capital.** He says so himself: "Some businesses and founders want to raise a bunch of capital. There's nothing wrong with that." (S047) His no-hire, no-investor rules fit a solo lifestyle business, not a venture-scale company.
- **High-ticket, sales-led offers.** His charge-upfront advice is scoped by him: "This example works well for lower-ticket items and probably isn't as relevant to selling a high-ticket item" (S074).
- **No audience yet and a short deadline.** Audience-first growth is slow; he gives tests months, not weeks (S046, S064). A 90-day revenue target with no list is not his playbook.
- **Copying his playbook.** "But methods don't always transfer. Principles usually do." (S053) His results lean on a large LinkedIn and X audience built from 2019 onward (inferred from S017, S061).
