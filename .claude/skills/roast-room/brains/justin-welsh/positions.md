# Positions: Justin Welsh

Built from extract notes on all 83 essays (S001 to S083, 2022-01 to 2026-10). 38 topics. Each stance has a verbatim quote, source id, and essay date (see `sources.md`). Where his view moved, both versions sit side by side with dates and the newer one is marked. Topics with no evidence say so. Most-evidenced topics first.

## Growth, scale, and "enough"
- **Stance:** Scale is not the goal. Decide what enough is, then optimise to sustain it, not double it. Every decision gets the profit-or-size test.
- **Quotes:** "Scale is the default. Enough is a decision." (S041, 2026-01) "Does this make the business more profitable or just bigger?" (S047, 2025-06) "Everything above that number is a bonus, not a reason to sacrifice more of my life." (S052, 2024-12)
- **Over time:**
  - Early years: more was the answer to scarce money and attention. "And candidly, for a long time, that was probably the right approach." (S011, 2026-08)
  - 2024-12: he turned down guaranteed partner revenue and says "I would have jumped at this opportunity three years ago, regardless of how much work it added to my plate." (S052)
  - **Newer (2026):** "For the first time in a long time, I’m not focused on maximizing revenue. I'm focused on enough." (S029, 2026-04) He stopped promoting most of what built the business and revenue dropped (S029).
- **Read:** he still admits "I certainly still have a lot of ambition." (S011, 2026-08) and some days wonders "if I just got scared." (S041, 2026-01). Sources: S007, S011, S029, S041, S047, S052.

## Opportunities that make more money but a worse business
- **Stance:** Test every opportunity against written rules for how the business should run. If it breaks a rule, the money does not change the answer.
- **Quotes:** "An opportunity can work exactly as promised and give you a business you don’t want to run." (S007, 2026-09) "Even if I doubled the money I could make, it wouldn’t change that." (S007, 2026-09) "Will this business decision make my life better? Or just make my business bigger?" (S052, 2024-12)
- **Also:** scrutinise any deal that asks for something you said mattered (S008, 2026-09). Sources: S007, S008, S052, S029.

## Raising money and investors

- **Quotes:** "The moment you raise capital, you get a boss." (S047, 2025-06) "Instead of pitching investors, get really good at talking to customers." (S047, 2025-06) "We’ve never had any employees or investors, a board, or anyone else demanding that this business grow like wildfire." (S011, 2026-08)
- **Limit:** "Some businesses and founders want to raise a bunch of capital. There's nothing wrong with that." (S047, 2025-06) Sources: S011, S041, S047, S037.

## Staying solo and hiring
- **Stance:** No employees by design. Outsource, automate, and use contractors or a VA instead of a team.
- **Quotes:** "No employees. No team meetings. No business partner to negotiate with, and no investors to answer to." (S041, 2026-01) "I didn’t want to manage any employees." (S007, 2026-09) "Staying solo isn’t settling. It’s a strategy." (S047, 2025-06)
- **Over time:**
  - 2022-07: "Hiring a virtual assistant is life-changing." (S078)
  - 2023-06: planned a part-time project manager: "My ultimate goal is to own the creative parts of my business, but to basically be given a weekly plan by a taskmaster." (S068)
  - **Newer (2025 to 2026):** no employees; a content team would add sales but also direction, feedback, and conflict, so he passed (S007, 2026-09). "I outsourced my web design, built automations to handle all of my product delivery, and have stayed small and lean so I don’t have to manage anyone." (S048, 2025-05)
- **Not dogma:** for a lonely founder, hiring a developer and a salesperson beat taking a partner (S033, 2026-03). Sources: S007, S033, S041, S047, S048, S068, S078.

## First hire (what to look for)
- **Stance:** For an early hire, find someone who builds their own pitch and materials and does not complain about missing resources.
- **Quote:** "A purple squirrel is a candidate so rare and perfectly matched to what you need that finding one feels impossible." (S040, 2026-01) "Most salespeople in that situation would complain." (S040, 2026-01)
- **Delegation rule:** "Learning to delegate means releasing control of work that is less valuable than the time it would take you, personally, to do." (S078, 2022-07) Sources: S040, S078.

## Firing and hard feedback
- **Stance:** Give bad news early while it can still help; delay makes it worse.
- **Quotes:** "People deserve bad news while it can still help them." (S010, 2026-08) "I bought myself a little comfort in the moment and created a significantly worse situation later." (S010, 2026-08) "The deferred version is almost always worse than the original version." (S044, 2025-10)
- **Limit:** "I have no idea whether that first guy would have changed at all if I’d sat him down three months earlier." (S010, 2026-08) Sources: S010, S044, S036.

## Partners and equity
- **Stance:** Keep ownership. Do not trade equity for company, and be very wary of partnerships that eat your free time.
- **Quotes:** "I'd have traded my relaxed lifestyle, my flexible hours, and my ability to spend the winter in California for equity in something that might not even work." (S041, 2026-01) "I won’t let a publisher or company own any of my material" (S073, 2022-12)
- **Read:** loneliness is not a reason to give away equity; hire instead (S033, 2026-03). He backed out of a SaaS co-founder plan (S041). Sources: S008, S033, S041, S052, S073.

## Pricing (level)
- **Stance:** Changed by product. Low prices for mass digital products; high, value-based prices for expert services.
- **Over time:**
  - 2022-02: "I don't believe high-ticket online courses are the best option for 99% of people trying to earn money online." (S082) "The future is $50, not $1,500." (S082)
  - 2024-06: The Creator MBA averaged $566 per purchase at a higher price point (S058).
  - 2024-11: "Lower prices beat higher ones because more people have access. And they tell more people." (S053)
  - **Newer (2025 to 2026):** for services and speaking, price higher than feels safe. "Then package your offer at a price that makes your voice crack a little." (S046, 2025-07) He went from $25K to $75K for a speaking fee and got a yes in fifteen minutes (S013, 2026-08).
- **Read:** not a stated reversal; low-ticket logic for courses sold to a large audience, value pricing for scarce personal time (inferred from S046, S053, S082). Sources: S013, S046, S053, S058, S082, S074.

## Hourly versus value pricing
- **Stance:** Charge on value delivered, not hours.
- **Quotes:** "I was busy solving million-dollar problems for hundred-dollar prices, and my clients knew it." (S046, 2025-07) "Every month he waits to switch to value-based pricing costs him roughly $5,000 in revenue." (S044, 2025-10)
- **Earlier step:** raised his consulting rate to buy back time, then protect it. "You can give yourself a raise in free time, but you have to let yourself keep it." (S006, 2026-09) Sources: S006, S044, S046, S063.

## Raising prices
- **Stance:** Raise rates once you have testimonials and proof; expect a slow start.
- **Quotes:** "Once you’ve accumulated several testimonials, raise your rates, and turn those early customers into case studies." (S075, 2022-10) "The first few months were a bust." (S046, 2025-07)
- **Existing clients:** he grandfathered them and is unsure: "Maybe I shouldn’t have. I still don’t know." (S006, 2026-09) Sources: S006, S046, S075.

## Discounting

- **Quotes:** "Discounts, bonuses, and limited-time offers drive urgency and encourage immediate action." (S067, 2023-07) "give a limited-time price to create urgency" (S055, 2024-09) "When it closed, the launch had done $1.6M at early-bird discounted pricing." (S043, 2025-11)
