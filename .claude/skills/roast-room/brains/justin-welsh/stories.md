# Stories: Justin Welsh

Built from extract notes on all 83 essays (S001 to S083). 20 stories and cases he reuses, with the lesson he draws. Source ids in parentheses. Most-used first.

1. **Fired three times, then ZocDoc.** Fired from his first sales jobs across Midwest towns, in credit card debt at 28 and sleeping on a friend's couch in Brooklyn, he answered a Craigslist ad for ZocDoc, took a job paying $18,000 less, and closed a doctor in about 30 minutes on day one. Cyrus Massoumi kept picking him for bigger roles. Lesson: change the conditions before deciding you are bad at something, and one person can redirect a career. "Turns out, I was just one job away from a brand new story." (S005) (S005, S011, S021, S030, S032, S039)
2. **PatientPop from one salesperson to $70M.** He left ZocDoc for an unfunded company with one salesperson and helped build it past $70 million in revenue, then found nobody online knew him. Lesson: a record does not speak for itself; you have to promote your thinking. "I figured that work should speak for itself. But guess what?" (S059) (S021, S036, S040, S059)
3. **Raising rates to keep Fridays clear.** As a healthcare consultant he raised his rate, narrowed to an ideal customer, and kept the freed hours instead of refilling them; that time became his first course. Lesson: give yourself a raise in time, then protect it. "And it all started by simply trying to keep my Fridays clear." (S006) (S006, S040)
4. **The ugly $50 course.** In spring 2020, with about 21,000 LinkedIn followers, he made The LinkedIn Playbook with a webcam and his own slides, priced it at $50, and launched with one post: $300 on day one, $10,482 in month one. The relaunch as The LinkedIn OS made over $4.5M. Lesson: build the ugliest version to learn what to build (S040, S006).
5. **Daily posting before the break.** In 2019 he posted on LinkedIn daily, did hundreds of podcasts, and sent thousands of DMs; seven months in, one April 2020 post booked thirty calls in a week, though his consulting skill had not improved. A smarter friend, Ben, kept polishing frameworks and applying for jobs. Lesson: past basic competence, visibility beats sharpening the axe (S017, S061, S068).
6. **Written non-negotiables and the $4M peak.** In 2020 he wrote six rules for the business (no employees, no more than 30 hours, most Fridays off, few short meetings). The business peaked at over $4M profit in 2024; readers keep saying ads, a team, or events would add a million, and he passes. Lesson: an opportunity that works can still give you a business you do not want (S007, S052).
7. **Walking away from guaranteed partner revenue.** In late 2024 he declined guaranteed annual revenue from a partner business he admired because of added work and Slack noise, which he says he would have taken three years earlier. Lesson: choose your life over a bigger business when they conflict (S052, S008).
8. **The 7x consulting price.** After the FITI process (feedback, iteration, testimonials, increase) he quoted $14,400 for eight hours a month, his voice cracked, and the CEO asked whether that was all. Three months brought no client; by month six he had four at the new rate. Lesson: price on value and expect a messy middle. "Your clients already know what you're worth. They're just waiting for you to figure it out." (S046) (S046)
9. **Content OS flop, Creator MBA win.** The Content Operating System in 2022 was expected to do $200K to $250K and did about $55K because he winged the launch. The Creator MBA used a signup survey and segmented emails and did about $1.6M, with personalization worth about $650,000. Lesson: a good product needs an equally careful launch (S043, S058).
10. **The one-page sponsorship system.** Instead of decks, meetings, and negotiations, he and Jennifer built one page with price, rules, a buy button, and a calendar; it ran unchanged from about 35,000 to 200,000+ readers. Lesson: try the simplest version first and refuse custom work that makes the business harder to run (S004).
11. **Backing out of the SaaS co-founder plan.** After months of planning a SaaS company with a co-founder, he pictured the team, office, and stress and saw the career he had left. Lesson: small can be a conscious choice, though he admits he may just have been scared (S041).
12. **Rejoining PatientPop for the title.** In 2021 he went back to PatientPop for eight months, missed the CRO title rather than the work, and returned to his own business relieved. Lesson: ask why you want something before chasing it (S033).
13. **Salesforce and staying too long.** In 2013 he knew within two weeks that Salesforce was wrong and stayed six months to avoid offending the person who introduced him. Lesson: you can just stop something that is not working (S036).
14. **Shutting down the revenue engine.** He discontinued The LinkedIn OS and Content OS, about 75% of revenue, after a year of preparation, and moved from tactics to personal essays; one essay about his garage drew over 500 replies against an average of about 45. Lesson: when obligation replaces curiosity, follow the curiosity even at a revenue cost (S027, S033, S035, S042).
15. **Website redesigns criticised, numbers up.** His 2023 and 2026 site redesigns drew loud criticism on Twitter; the 2026 one lifted new subscribers 49.1% in under three weeks. Lesson: let chosen numbers, not fast opinions, decide (S020).
16. **Blake Burge's $2,243 thread.** In a 30-minute call he told an Excel creator to sell a $10 template at the end of a thread instead of giving it away and nurturing; the thread made 76 times his average. Lesson: remove friction and offer at peak interest, for low-ticket items (S074).
17. **Scott Bair's weekly reminders.** A reader considered The LinkedIn Operating System for over a year and bought after weekly reminders; ask days made 5x to 10x passive days. Lesson: ask for the business repeatedly. "How many Scotts are out there waiting to buy your product?" (S083) (S083)
18. **Patrick's agency gutted by AI.** A friend with 21 employees and $5,000-a-month content clients lost them to AI and cheaper rivals, admitted he never liked the work, and rebuilt a lean version. Lesson: know your enough, or the machine runs you (S029).
19. **Craig's coaching practice died of freedom.** A top SDR leader left to coach in 2020, travelled, stopped marketing, and his practice was dead within two years. Lesson: freedom without a schedule starves the pipeline (S025).
20. **The Director he fired too late.** In 2017 he avoided a hard talk with a Director of Inside Sales for months, then fired him; a year later he gave a top performer early, direct feedback and the man grew into a leader. Lesson: people deserve bad news while it can still help them (S010).
