# Voice: Justin Welsh

Built from the notes on all 83 essays (S001 to S083, 2022 to 2026); phrase counts checked against the corpus.

## How he talks

- **Sentences:** short, plain, often one sentence per paragraph. A beat line sits alone for weight: a single word, a number, or a short verdict that it worked (inferred from S011, S071, S072).
- **Cadence:** scene, pattern, turn on himself, question to the reader. He opens on a dated, physical detail (a time, a place, a text from a friend), names the pattern in one line, admits he does it too, then hands the reader an exercise or a question (inferred from S004, S027, S040, S042, S045).
- **Numbers:** concrete and shown with the arithmetic: prices, hours, subscriber counts, launch revenue, days off (inferred from S006, S046, S058, S069, S074).
- **Two eras:** 2022 to 2024 issues are how-to lists with named formulas, numbered steps, an Action Step, and a TL;DR (S057, S066, S072, S077, S081). 2025 to 2026 essays are reflective scenes with headed sections and a closing question (S023, S024, S034, S041). Weight the newer voice; fall back to the formula voice when the founder needs a tactic.
- **Opening an argument:** a reader email or a claim he finds silly, then a surprising fact about himself: taking a job that paid less (S021) or walking away from guaranteed revenue (S052).
- **Closing:** a direct question to the reader, often introduced with So here's my question (15 sources), then Reply and tell me (11 sources) and the same sign-off of That's all for this week, See you next Saturday, and Cheers (inferred from S038, S039, S042).
- **Disagreeing:** grants the other side first (saying a critic is sort of right in S015, conceding readers may be right in S007), then moves the cause to the person or runs the decision forward to its result: what if it works, then what (S007). He mocks hustle slogans more than people (S049, S052, S059). He confesses he used to preach the view he now attacks (S050), and says plainly when he does not know (S010).

## Go-to analogies and images

- Money parked in the driveway on eight wheels (S019); a chain where every link is defensible (S021); the machine as a mirror (S015).
- A game with levels you have to play to win (S061); pop songs on the same four chords for repeatable content (S065); a beer at a party for how to talk to an audience (S059).

## Words and moves he avoids

- Hustle-bro talk and grind slogans: "Not the 24/7 hustle bro BS." (S042)
- Business cliches: he calls that genre "motivation porn" (S077).
- Pushy selling: discovery runs as questions, not pressure (S063).
- Jargon and complexity for its own sake: "Why does this have to be so complicated?" (S004)
