## Why the council landed here

- $2,000 caps the goal before demand gets a vote. At 13 to 15 hours an install, the $5k target takes 2.5 installs a month at $2,000, about 31 to 37 of a 43-hour month. $3,500 needs 1.4 installs and about 18 to 21 hours. (Martell, Buyback Rate; Welsh, Try the easy version first)
- Once 4 hours of tuning are counted, $2,000 pays about $121 an hour, below consulting at any rate the seats assumed. Service pricing should be 5 to 10x delivery cost; this is 0.8x. (Hormozi, 5 to 10x delivery cost; Graham conceded his own figure)

- The $500 tier is leverage, but only after there is something to write down. Record installs 1 to 3 and build the runbook from real work. (Naval, Three forms of leverage; Martell, Camcorder Method)

## Where they fought

Graham moved in Round 2 when the capacity math showed B cannot reach the goal even with perfect demand. **Is the $500 self-serve tier leverage now?** Naval said yes, as the only line that earns without the founder's hours. Everyone else said not yet: no install exists to document, and there is no audience to sell it to. Naval moved to "A now, C later", and the room adopted his kill test for C.

**Does $2,000 already beat consulting per hour?** Graham said yes at $160 an hour. Hormozi, Naval and Welsh showed the figure left out tuning. Buffett conceded that break-even sits at 13.3 hours, so the hours log matters. In Round 3 Graham showed the log does not change the call: at 13 hours, capacity alone justifies the raise. The log now sets the hourly case and the tuning cap, not the go decision.
