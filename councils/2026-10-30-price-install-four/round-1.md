# Round 1: opening positions

## Hormozi

SEAT: Hormozi (Offers, pricing, and sales)
STANCE: Conditional yes
OPTION SCORES: A 8/10 · B 3/10 · C 2/10
PILLAR SCORE: 7/10
CONFIDENCE: 3/5
RUIN RISK: none
DOOR: two-way

THE CALL: Take A, $3,500 with a stated result guarantee, announced now and dated for December, unless installs 1 to 3 close under 30% of sales conversations at $2,000.

WHY:
1. The math says $2,000 loses money against your own time (5 to 10x delivery cost, S094). I'm assuming your consulting rate is about $150 an hour. At 12.5 hours plus 4 hours of tuning, delivery costs about $2,500 of your time. $2,000 is under cost. Even $3,500 is only 1.4x cost, which is nowhere near 5x. You are not overpriced at $3,500. You are still cheap.
2. Judge by close rate times price, not by gut (Price raising rules, S015, S021). Say $2,000 closes at 40%: that's $800 per conversation. $3,500 only has to close above 23% to win. "I encourage everyone to continue to raise prices until your conversion rate times price equal less money" (S015).
3. C is a new product, built for the cheap middle, by one guy with 10 hours a week (111 rule, S059; More Better New, S070). A $500 runbook gets you $500 a head. To hit $5k a month you'd need 10 of them, and each one still eats a 60-minute call plus the support tickets. "Either sell extremely expensive stuff to a select few or sell something super cheap to everyone. The middle is where people die." (S050). Hold C back as a downsell for prospects who say no to A. Don't launch it as a second offer.

THE RISK I SEE: you raise on zero delivered installs, so you have no proof, no testimonials and no hours log. The extra $1,500 then has to be carried by a promise you've never kept.

WHAT WOULD CHANGE MY MIND: if fewer than 3 of the 20 consultants you message book a call at $2,000, the constraint is leads, not price. Then neither A nor C fixes it.

WHAT I'D DO THIS WEEK: write the A guarantee in the form "if you don't X in Y time, we will Z", for example "20 usable ideas a week by day 30 or I keep tuning free" (Guarantees, S115). Put it in front of the next 5 prospects with the date the price goes up.

QUESTION FOR THE FOUNDER: of the people you've pitched at $2,000, what percentage said yes, and how many did you pitch?

## Graham

SEAT: Graham (Product and startups)
STANCE: Conditional yes
OPTION SCORES: A 4/10 · B 7/10 · C 3/10
PILLAR SCORE: 4/10
CONFIDENCE: 3/5
RUIN RISK: none
DOOR: two-way

THE CALL: B, kept at $2,000 for installs 4 to 6, with a written trigger: raise to $3,500 once three paid installs close inside 30 days and logged hours come in under 12 per install.

WHY:
1. Nobody has bought yet. The founder wants to price install #4 while zero installs have shipped. On demand evidence this scores 1 of 5: what we have is the founder's own enthusiasm, not usage (Make something people want, S149). You can't reprice a market you haven't met.
2. Raising later is cheap. Cutting a price that stalled sales costs you the month it stalled. "you can always change your prices later though if you want to lower your prices no one's gonna complain" (S009). Newer view: "sell as cheaply as they need to at first; get all the users, then worry about your margins." (S019)
3. The hourly argument is missing a number. $2,000 over 12.5 hours is $160 an hour. Consulting is $15k a month, but the brief doesn't say for how many hours. At 100 hours that's $150 an hour, and the install already pays more. Log install 1's hours before quoting the gap.
4. C is a second product before the first one works. Doing it by hand is how you learn what the runbook should say (Do things that don't scale, S096). You can't write a good runbook until you've done three installs.
I'll concede that this is a services offer, not a startup, so the growth reflex is a weaker guide here and Hormozi's pricing lens deserves real weight.

THE RISK I SEE: the first three installs close slowly or don't close at all, and a $3,500 quote in December hides whether the problem is the price or the product.

WHAT WOULD CHANGE MY MIND: 3 installs closed by 2026-11-30 off 20 messages, with 2 or more prospects asking for it without being pushed. That's demand pulling, and I'd move to A.

WHAT I'D DO THIS WEEK: send the 20 messages and count replies, calls and closes in a table.

QUESTION FOR THE FOUNDER: of the 3 installs, how many buyers came to you asking, and how many did you have to persuade?

## Naval

SEAT: Naval (Leverage and wealth)
STANCE: Yes
OPTION SCORES: A 4/10 · B 2/10 · C 8/10
PILLAR SCORE: 7/10
CONFIDENCE: 3/5
RUIN RISK: none
DOOR: two-way

THE CALL: Option C. Keep the $2,000 install as the premium tier, and treat the $500 runbook as the real product, because it is the only line here that earns while you sleep.

WHY:
1. A and B both rent your time. I'm assuming consulting runs about 120 hours a month at $15k, so roughly $125 an hour. At $2,000 for about 13 hours plus tuning, B pays under that. At $3,500, A pays about $230 an hour. That's a better rent, but it's still rent. "You’re not going to get rich renting out your time." (S024)
2. C is the first use of permissionless leverage in this plan. The runbook is media, so writing it once costs a few hours and copying it costs nothing. The plugin is code. One 60-minute call is the only labor left. "Leverage comes in labor, comes in capital, or it can come through code or media." (S001) At about 1.5 hours per sale, $500 works out to roughly $330 an hour, and that rate goes up every time the runbook replaces a question you used to answer live.
3. Two tiers is sound price discrimination, if the difference between them is real. "Price discrimination is important. It means you can charge people based on their propensity to pay." (S001) The serious buyer gets your hands on the install. Everyone else gets your written judgment. That's productizing yourself (S001).

THE RISK I SEE: The $500 tier becomes unpaid support. Clients run the install, hit errors with their API keys, and your one call becomes five. Then you've built a cheaper way to rent your time.

WHAT WOULD CHANGE MY MIND: If the first three self-serve clients each need more than 3 hours of your time, C is labor wearing a product costume, and A becomes the better rent.

WHAT I'D DO THIS WEEK: During install #1, record every step and every fix as you go, so the runbook writes itself out of real work and not out of memory.

QUESTION FOR THE FOUNDER: If nobody ever paid you for the done-for-you install again, would you still want to own this runbook and plugin?

Own the recipe, not the kitchen hours.

## Welsh

SEAT: Welsh (Solo business and content-led growth)
STANCE: Conditional yes
OPTION SCORES: A 7/10 · B 3/10 · C 4/10
PILLAR SCORE: 7/10
CONFIDENCE: 3/5
RUIN RISK: none
DOOR: two-way

THE CALL: Go with A at $3,500 from install #4, but only if installs 1 to 3 each produce a testimonial with a hard number. Hold C until the runbook already exists as a side effect of the first three installs.

WHY:
1. Your 10 hours a week sets the ceiling, not demand. That is roughly 43 hours a month. At 12.5 hours per install (my midpoint of your 10 to 15), hitting $5k a month at $2,000 takes 2.5 installs, which is about 31 hours plus tuning. Your whole budget is gone. At $3,500 it takes 1.4 installs, about 18 hours. Option B makes the business harder to run, and "It couldn’t make the business harder to run." (Try the easy version first, S004)
2. You get the proof first, then the raise. "Once you’ve accumulated several testimonials, raise your rates, and turn those early customers into case studies." (FITI method, S075) Three installs is a thin signal for demand. It is enough proof for a price.
3. C is a second product with a 60-minute call attached. To reach $5k a month it needs 10 sales, which is 10 calls plus support tickets. It also needs attention you don't have: nothing in the brief points to an email list. As a tripwire it only works once there is an audience to sell to (Trust tripwire and value ladder, S082). Later it makes a good anchor next to A (5-4-3-2-1, S057). Today it's a distraction.

THE RISK I SEE: December goes quiet at the new price and you panic back to $2,000. That happened at a raise before. "The first few months were a bust." (S046)

WHAT WOULD CHANGE MY MIND: You close fewer than 3 of the 20 consultants at $2,000 by 2026-11-30. Then you have no proof, and B stays.

WHAT I'D DO THIS WEEK: Add a day-30 testimonial ask to every install agreement. It asks for one number, such as ideas shipped or hours saved, and you log your own hours next to it.

QUESTION FOR THE FOUNDER: How many installs a month do you actually want to deliver? Have you written that number down?

## Martell

SEAT: Martell (People, hiring, and founder time)
STANCE: Yes
OPTION SCORES: A 8/10 · B 2/10 · C 6/10
PILLAR SCORE: 8/10
CONFIDENCE: 3/5
RUIN RISK: none
DOOR: two-way

THE CALL: Go with A, $3,500, and record installs 1 to 3 on camera so the runbook for C already exists by spring.

WHY:
1. Calendar math (Buyback Rate, S018). You have 10 hours a week, about 43 a month. I'm assuming 15 hours per install once tuning is counted. At $2,000, the $5k goal takes 2.5 installs a month. That's 37.5 hours, or 87% of your capacity, which leaves about 5 hours to sell. At $3,500 it takes 1.4 installs, about 21 hours, and you get half your week back to sell. B fails before demand even gets a vote.
2. You're undercharging, and you know it (Pricing, S056). "Most people do not charge enough for their software or their products in their business or services. Period. Full stop." If $15k a month comes from about 80 billable hours, consulting pays around $187 an hour, and B pays $133. Then use the 10-5-20 rule (S089). If nobody pushes back at $3,500, that's your signal to go higher.
3. C is the right move, just too early (Camcorder Method, S020; Four C's, S007). A $500 runbook only works once a stranger can get 70 to 80% of the way from the documents alone (S097). Nobody has done an install yet, so there's nothing to document. Record installs 1 to 3 while you deliver them. "I literally record myself doing the work while I'm doing the work so it takes me no extra time." That's how C gets built at no extra cost.

THE RISK I SEE: The "30 days of tuning" has no definition of done. If it's open-ended, a $3,500 client eats 25 hours and you're back below your consulting rate.

WHAT WOULD CHANGE MY MIND: If install 1's log (due 2026-11-13) shows 8 hours or less including tuning, B is fine on hours and the argument for raising becomes purely about price. If it shows over 20 hours, $3,500 isn't enough either.

WHAT I'D DO THIS WEEK: Write a definition of done for tuning: three named deliverables, two check-in calls, a cap on hours. Start the screen recorder on install 1.

QUESTION FOR THE FOUNDER: If installs give you 20 hours a month back, what exactly will you put in them: selling installs, consulting, or building C?
