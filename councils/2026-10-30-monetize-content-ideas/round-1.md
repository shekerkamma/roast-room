# Round 1: opening positions

## Hormozi

SEAT: HORMOZI (Offers, pricing, and sales)
STANCE: Strong yes
OPTION SCORES: A 5/10 · B 2/10 · C 9/10 · D 1/10
PILLAR SCORE: 8/10
CONFIDENCE: 4/5
RUIN RISK: none
DOOR: two-way

THE CALL: Run C, the $2,000 done-for-you install, and use A's free plugin as the lead magnet that feeds it. Kill B for this 90-day window.

WHY:
1. The math on B doesn't work. $5k a month at $29 is 173 paying users. My assumption is that ScrapeCreators costs about $10 per user, which leaves $19 of gross profit, so you need about 263 subscribers. With about 8% monthly SMB churn and no audience, you won't get there in 90 days on 10 hours a week. "If you want to win at software, you have to commit to making no money for basically seven years. Zero." (Business types, S034)
2. C hits the target with 3 sales a month: 3 x $2,000 = $6k. Your CAC is close to zero because you'd sell to your consulting network, and you get the cash upfront. That clears "30-day cash must be greater than two times the cost of acquiring customer and the cost of fulfilling that customer." (Client-financed acquisition, S037) I'm assuming each install takes about 8 hours, which is 24 hours a month and fits inside your 40.
3. C is the strongest offer on the Value Equation (S115). The client gets the outcome, they don't do the setup, and they see a result in days. A $29 self-serve plan leaves all the setup and tuning effort on the buyer, and that kills value.
4. The free plugin works as attraction: "A lead magnet is a complete solution to a narrow problem." (Lead magnets, S112) Its next problem ("I can't set this up") is exactly what C sells. After the 30 days, add a $300 a month tuning retainer as continuity. Three clients a quarter at $300 is the recurring revenue you wanted from B, and it doesn't carry B's churn risk.

THE RISK I SEE: Installs turn into custom consulting. You end up spending 20 hours per client, and it eats into the consulting hours you said you wouldn't cut.

WHAT WOULD CHANGE MY MIND: You pitch C to 15 warm contacts and fewer than 2 buy. Or 50 or more people prepay $29 for B before you build it.

WHAT I'D DO THIS WEEK: Write a one-page C offer with a fixed scope, a 5-day install, and a guarantee of 30 usable ideas in the first week. Send it to 20 consultants and agency owners you already know.

QUESTION FOR THE FOUNDER: How many people who already pay you, or already know you, would take a sales call about this next week?

## Graham

SEAT: GRAHAM (Product and startups)
STANCE: Conditional yes
OPTION SCORES: A 3/10 · B 4/10 · C 7/10 · D 1/10
PILLAR SCORE: 6/10
CONFIDENCE: 3/5
RUIN RISK: none
DOOR: two-way

THE CALL: Sell C by hand to five named consultants first, and treat every install as a test of whether B deserves to exist.

WHY:
1. Nobody has paid yet. The only evidence of demand right now is the founder's own enthusiasm, and that is the lowest score on my scale. Do the arithmetic for B: $5k a month at $29 means 173 paying subscribers in 90 days, starting from zero, with no list I can see. I'd bet against that. (Make something people want, S149)
2. C is the unscalable version of B, and that is a good thing. Installing it yourself is the Collison installation: you sit next to the user and watch what they actually use. "I have never once seen a startup lured down a blind alley by trying too hard to make their initial users happy." (Do things that don't scale, S096) Three installs a month at $2,000 is $6k, which clears the target. I'm assuming about 12 founder hours per install, so three a month fits inside 10 hours a week only if the tuning is light.
3. Start narrow. Consultants and small agencies who already sell content work are a group who might want this badly. "You got to start with a small intense fire." (Small intense fire, S010) A $29 plan aimed at everyone is a large group wanting it a little.
4. Pricing is not the hard part. "you can always change your prices later though if you want to lower your prices no one's gonna complain" (S009) So don't agonize over $29 versus $2,000. Find out who pays at all.

THE RISK I SEE: C turns into a second consulting line and the product never gets built. "Consulting is where product companies go to die." (S194) The installs must feed B, not replace it.

WHAT WOULD CHANGE MY MIND: If 3 of the first 5 C buyers are still opening the feed daily at day 30 and ask to keep paying monthly, B is real. If fewer than 2 of 10 people pitched will pay $2,000, nobody wants a paid version yet.

WHAT I'D DO THIS WEEK: Write down 10 consultants by name, message each one personally, and try to close two installs by Friday.

QUESTION FOR THE FOUNDER: Name one person outside yourself who uses the feed every morning and would be upset if it went away. Who is it?

## Buffett

SEAT: BUFFETT (Money and capital)
STANCE: Yes
OPTION SCORES: A 6/10 · B 3/10 · C 8/10 · D 2/10
PILLAR SCORE: 8/10
CONFIDENCE: 4/5
RUIN RISK: none
DOOR: two-way

THE CALL: Run C, the $2,000 done-for-you install, on the client's own ScrapeCreators key, and open B only after ten C clients ask you to host it.

WHY:
1. Birds in the bush (S011): "how much do you get back, how sure are you of getting it, when do you get it?" With C, three installs a month brings in $6,000, paid up front, from buyers who already know the founder. With B, you need 173 subscribers at $29 to reach $5,017, starting from zero in 90 days. That is about two new payers a day on 10 hours a week. I'd take one bird in the hand over 173 in the bush.
2. Great, good, and gruesome (S080): "The worst sort of business is one that grows rapidly, requires significant capital to engender the growth, and then earns little or no money." On B, every user adds an API bill. I'm assuming $8 a user, plus about 4% in payment fees and $100 of hosting. That leaves roughly $3,350 net at 173 users, before churn. Support works out to 3.5 minutes per user per month. On C the client pays for the API, so each dollar of growth needs $0 of my capital.
3. Second-best idea (S013): "our best idea has to exceed that." The next-best use of the founder's hours is consulting. At $15k a month over an assumed 120 hours, that pays $125 an hour. C at 12 hours per install pays about $167 an hour, and each install makes a warm consulting lead. That is my translation of the rule to an operating business. A keeps that lead flow but brings in no cash you can credit to the plugin.
THE RISK I SEE: Each install takes 25 hours instead of 12, the 10-hour budget runs out, and consulting hours quietly pay for it.
WHAT WOULD CHANGE MY MIND: 50 people paying for B in advance at $29 within three weeks, with a measured API cost under $5 per user.
WHAT I'D DO THIS WEEK: Offer the C install to 20 consultants in your network and log the hours on the first one.
QUESTION FOR THE FOUNDER: What does one user's ScrapeCreators bill actually come to in a month of daily feeds?

## Naval

SEAT: NAVAL (Leverage and wealth)
STANCE: Conditional yes (on B)
OPTION SCORES: A 5/10 · B 7/10 · C 4/10 · D 2/10
PILLAR SCORE: 7/10
CONFIDENCE: 3/5
RUIN RISK: none
DOOR: two-way

THE CALL: B, with a change: scrape each competitor handle once and serve that same data to every subscriber who tracks it, so the cost per user falls as you add users and the feed becomes a shared dataset you own.

WHY:
1. Only B is code leverage. A and C still pay you in hours. I'm assuming consulting is about 100 hours a month, which makes your hour worth about $150. At that rate C's $2,000 install plus 30 days of tuning is consulting under another name, and A just brings in more consulting. "Leverage comes in labor, comes in capital, or it can come through code or media." (Three kinds of leverage, S001)
2. Ownership is the goal, even though B is the slowest way to $5k a month. You need about 172 subscribers at $29 to get there. C gets there with 3 installs a month, but income from C stops when you stop working. "You’re not going to get rich renting out your time." (Own equity, S024)
3. The repo is public, so the code itself is not your moat. "pure software is uninvestable. I would just full stop right there." (Where the moat moved, S006) What you can own is the cached performance data across users plus your judgment about which ideas to feature. That shared data also fixes your margin worry. I'm assuming $5 to $10 a month in ScrapeCreators cost per user before sharing. Once 10 users track the same creator, that cost is split 10 ways.

THE RISK I SEE: You build a hosted wrapper that no one pays for. That would use up 130 hours you could have billed at about $150 each, roughly $19k.

WHAT WOULD CHANGE MY MIND: If fewer than 20 people prepay within 30 days, I'd switch to C as paid discovery. I'd also switch if scraping still costs more than $10 per user after sharing.

WHAT I'D DO THIS WEEK: Put a $29-a-month founding-plan page with a live payment button in front of your consulting network and your content audience. Then count the payments. "Free markets are harsh: either people buy it, or they don’t." (Feedback from reality, S029)

QUESTION FOR THE FOUNDER: Your repo is public, so what does your feed know that someone's agent couldn't rebuild from it in a weekend?
